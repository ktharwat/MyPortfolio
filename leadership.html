Leadership

Leadership is a delivery mechanism.

Titles don't ship software; decisions do — and the culture that produces them. This is how I lead, and what it has produced.

01

Own the outcome, not the ticket.

When I took over delivery ownership at Eva Pharma, the first thing I changed wasn't tooling — it was decision rights. Every engineer knows which calls are theirs, which are mine, and which belong to product. Escalation became the exception instead of the default.

The measurable part: +30% team productivity through structured code reviews and mentoring. The invisible part: stakeholders aligned early enough that escalation stopped being necessary.

02

Modernize in production, never big-bang.

I have spent a career inheriting systems other teams were afraid to touch. Big-bang rewrites fail politically before they fail technically — so I migrate behind stable contracts: strangler-style service extraction, OAuth/JWT at the edge, Redis caching in front of slow paths, and Swagger/OpenAPI so every consumer sees the same truth.

The business never stops while the system changes underneath it. That is the whole point.

03

Grow the people who grow the system.

A lead's real output isn't code — it's the team's capacity to absorb change. Structured code review, pairing on the hard problems, and clear growth signals turned nine engineers into a team that ships without anyone standing over them.

That mentoring program is exactly where the thirty percent came from.

In practice

What this looks like week to week.

  • Technical roadmap planning with trade-offs made explicit enough for stakeholders to challenge
  • Cross-functional collaboration across product, QA and infrastructure — one plan, not three
  • Sprint planning, review culture and mentoring scheduled as first-class work, not squeezed around it
  • Delivery management that treats deadlines as engineering data, not slogans
