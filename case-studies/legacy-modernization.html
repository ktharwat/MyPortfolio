Enterprise · NDA-safe · Technical Lead

Zero-downtime legacy modernization.

Inheriting a .NET estate nobody wanted to touch and moving it toward services, modern auth and measured performance — while the business kept running.

Employer and client details are generalized per confidentiality agreements. Numbers are my own measurements on systems I owned.

Context

An enterprise system that had grown organically for years: a monolith at its center, direct database access from every direction, authentication bolted on rather than designed in. Business demand for change had not slowed down to match.

Challenge

Every modification was risky because everything touched everything. The business needed monthly feature delivery; the architecture made each delivery feel like surgery without anesthesia.

Constraints

  • Feature work could not be frozen for a rewrite — ever
  • Data integrity was non-negotiable during and after migration
  • No budget for a parallel team; modernization had to ride alongside operations

My role

Technical lead of the modernization track: choosing what got extracted, in which order, behind which contracts — and defending the sequencing against pressure to "just rewrite it".

Decisions

Strangler extraction behind stable facades.

Capabilities left the monolith one at a time behind service boundaries. The old system shrank as the new one grew, and traffic shifted gradually instead of flipping on a date.

Authentication moved to the edge first.

OAuth/JWT in front of everything gave identity continuity even mid-migration. Every extracted service inherited a working auth story instead of inventing its own.

Redis caching in front of the slow paths we hadn't fixed yet.

Users felt improvement immediately while structural work continued underneath. Performance became a running result, not a launch-day promise.

Swagger/OpenAPI as the single source of interface truth.

Parallel teams stopped guessing about contracts. Documented-once interfaces ended the slowest argument in software: what does this endpoint actually return?

Execution

Capability by capability: extract, dual-run to verify parity, shift traffic, retire the old path. Data-layer redesign with Entity Framework and LINQ went hand-in-hand with query tuning. Heavy flows were decoupled through RabbitMQ messaging so failures could be isolated instead of contagious.

Results

+25%
data-retrieval performance after schema and query redesign
0
big-bang cutovers — every replacement happened gradually
100%
of traffic under unified OAuth/JWT authentication

Business impact

Feature velocity recovered because changes stopped being all-or-nothing. Operational risk fell with every retired legacy path. And the organization gained a repeatable pattern for modernization — not just one lucky migration.

Lessons

You cannot refactor what you cannot observe. Instrumentation came before everything else — and it was the cheapest decision of the entire effort.
