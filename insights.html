Modernizing systems that can't afford downtime.

KYRELLOS THARWAT · SOFTWARE DEVELOPMENT LEAD · ALEXANDRIA

Most legacy systems don't fail technically. They fail politically: the rewrite that demands an eighteen-month freeze, the vendor nobody can replace, the module only one semi-retired engineer understands. I have spent twelve years inheriting systems like these. Here is what actually works.

Five rules I migrate by

  1. Freeze contracts, not features. Stabilize APIs before touching implementations; every consumer keeps working while the internals change.
  2. Strangle, don't amputate. Extract one capability at a time behind a facade. The old system shrinks as the new one grows, and traffic shifts gradually.
  3. Put authentication at the edge first. OAuth/JWT in front of everything buys identity continuity even in the middle of a migration.
  4. Cache the slow paths you haven't fixed yet. Redis buys user-visible performance while structural work continues underneath it.
  5. Document the truth exactly once. Swagger/OpenAPI isn't paperwork — it's how parallel teams stop lying to each other about interfaces.

None of this is glamorous. All of it ships. And the business never stops — which was the requirement all along.
